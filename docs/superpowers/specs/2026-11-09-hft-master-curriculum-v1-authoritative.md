## 2. Design choices and learning promise

This is the owner-approved **55-class sequence: 8 existing + 47 new**. Stages 2–10 contain 44 new instructional classes; Capstone contains three assessment/workshop sessions, not three disguised lecture classes. Stage reviews and assessments are additional activities, not extra numbered classes. Calendar duration is not fixed: feedback, absence, practice and reassessment require additional time.

Alternatives considered: (a) a short survey with fewer broad classes, which overloads mechanics and practical assessment; (b) an exhaustive terminology/pattern catalog, which duplicates concepts and promotes recognition over usable process; (c) **owner-approved: bounded skill classes followed by integrated evidence**, with five-or-fewer classes in most stages and six in mechanics. Demi approved option (c) through the five owner policies recorded in Section 12.

Backward-design outcome: the graduate can independently **follow, explain, audit and improve a bounded trading process in simulation**, including not trading. This is not a promise of profitability, personal financial suitability, licensing, employment, funded-account eligibility or permission to risk real money. “Independent” means no instructor entry signals, with safe stopping/escalation when information is insufficient. Financial/Trading Readiness is separate from educational placement: income, debt, savings and trading capital must not determine Beginner/Developing/Intermediate/Advanced educational labels. Use fictional financial figures for assessment.

Learning progression: financial foundations → FX mechanics → chart literacy → structural analysis → qualified liquidity/context → contextual technical/macro synthesis → capital protection → plan/execution → psychology/discipline → testable personal system → independent process demonstration.

**Early guardrails, later depth:** no real-money requirement anywhere. Risk awareness and demo/live separation begin in Stage 2; uncertainty and no-action recur throughout. Numeric sizing and aggregate risk are taught in Stage 7 before any full simulated order-operation assignment. Journaling begins as an observation/decision record in Stage 3, becomes an execution journal in Stage 8, and a review/system-testing record later. Basic event/time awareness starts in Stage 2; macro scenario analysis waits until Stage 6. Candle and swing observation precede specialized labels. Optional live demonstrations never substitute for individual academic evidence.

## 4. Sequence, prerequisite and evidence conventions

Identifiers **S2.1 … S10.5 and C.1 … C.3 are document references only**. They are not installed IDs, new enum contracts or approved changes to existing identifiers. Each new class requires the immediately preceding listed class and its demonstrated competencies; the first class of each stage requires the last class of the prior stage. Additional recall dependencies appear on each card. Transitive prerequisites apply. Approved class competency labels below are human-friendly, not a substitute for future reviewed stable-ID mapping. **Deliberate Capstone exception:** C.3 is a correction/review session and is accessible after a recorded C.2 attempt and assessor debrief, even when criteria need revision. Access does not imply C.2 mastery or Capstone approval. Unresolved safety failures prohibit further affected simulated execution until targeted remediation and supervised fresh reassessment are arranged; discussion/correction remains accessible. Final approval requires every required criterion, including all safety criteria, demonstrated on fresh evidence.

Before authoring, each stage will map its approved competency labels to approved domain contracts or explicitly propose additive domains; do not silently repurpose Stage 1’s money domains. There is no claim that these mappings already exist. Detailed per-item IDs, tolerances and immutable academic versions belong to later authoring, within this approved scope if adopted.

**Approved evidence ladder:** conceptual knowledge; reasoned explanation; task application; practical demonstration; integrated review. Every class specifies what is required. Short responses complement diagrams/calculations rather than replacing them with essays. Deterministic tasks can be checked automatically against a private key; semantic reasoning and practical validity require individual instructor review, not AI or length-only grading. Live participation, attendance and profitable outcomes are not mastery evidence by themselves.

**Approved stage-readiness policy:** individual class requirements plus a fresh integrated stage packet, reviewed before the next stage. Stage 1’s existing rules remain unchanged. The future Stage 1→2 readiness process must use existing approved completion/evidence and instructor review; no unimplemented stage-exam gate may be assumed active. Approved stage gates need explicit future implementation authorization. Until then, instructor assignment remains explicit and no new automatic unlocking is implied.

### 2. `stage-2` — Forex Foundations

#### S2.1 — Currency Pairs and Exchange-Rate Meaning

- **Stage / sequence:** `stage-2`; class 1 of 6; Academy class 9 of 55.
- **Prerequisite classes/competencies:** S1.8 — Welcome to the Foreign Exchange Market (existing approved evidence; no new Stage 1 requirement). Explicit recall: S1.8 FX purpose; S1.4 arithmetic and buying-power comparisons.
- **Purpose:** Translate a real currency need into a correctly interpreted pair.
- **Learning objectives / competencies:** Construct base/quote relationships; interpret and invert a specified rate without reversing units. Demonstrate both with fresh material; terminology recognition alone is insufficient.
- **Core concepts:** Base and quote units; rate as quote units per one base unit; reciprocal interpretation; majors/crosses and qualified exotic labels.
- **Student demonstration:** Build a pair for an invoice, label units and explain what a rising quote changes for each currency.
- **Visual / interactive modes:** Pair-construction balance; unit-labeled conversion strips; predict then reveal.
- **Homework / practical work:** Build and interpret three pairs with one reversed quote and one unchanged currency need.
- **Targeted remediation:** Reversed numerator/denominator; direction without units; treating a currency as absolutely strong. Return to the failed skill and reassess with a changed example, preserving original attempts.
- **Future stage-assessment evidence:** Fresh pair-construction and quote-interpretation tasks, reasoning separate from arithmetic.
- **Live-teaching purpose:** Compare two students’ opposite currency needs and reconcile their descriptions. This is a substantive approximately 60-minute facilitated session, with individual follow-up outside it where required.
- **Explicitly deferred / excluded:** Pip value, sizing and execution; no economic forecast.

#### S2.2 — The FX Market, Participants and Trading Products

- **Stage / sequence:** `stage-2`; class 2 of 6; Academy class 10 of 55.
- **Prerequisite classes/competencies:** S2.1 — Currency Pairs and Exchange-Rate Meaning (the two competency outcomes above). Explicit recall: S1.5 markets; S1.8 FX purpose.
- **Purpose:** Distinguish the broad FX market from the particular product and counterparty used in a simulation.
- **Learning objectives / competencies:** Map participants to purposes; distinguish spot exchange, rolling retail products, forwards/swaps and exchange-traded futures at overview level. Demonstrate both with fresh material; terminology recognition alone is insufficient.
- **Core concepts:** OTC network; dealer/customer relationship; hedging versus speculation; counterparty; product terms; provider/feed differences.
- **Student demonstration:** Trace a payment and a speculative retail contract without claiming both are the same transaction or a central exchange.
- **Visual / interactive modes:** Participant ecosystem; compare term sheets; counterparty-route matching.
- **Homework / practical work:** Annotated product/purpose comparison using fictional providers and explicit unknowns.
- **Targeted remediation:** Confusing a chart/platform with an exchange; assigning dealer behavior to every venue. Return to the failed skill and reassess with a changed example, preserving original attempts.
- **Future stage-assessment evidence:** Classify unfamiliar participant/product cases and identify missing terms.
- **Live-teaching purpose:** Role-play exporter, dealer and retail customer with different obligations. This is a substantive approximately 60-minute facilitated session, with individual follow-up outside it where required.
- **Explicitly deferred / excluded:** Derivative pricing, arbitrage systems and detailed broker selection; advanced liquidity inference.

#### S2.3 — Bid, Ask, Spread and Trading Costs

- **Stage / sequence:** `stage-2`; class 3 of 6; Academy class 11 of 55.
- **Prerequisite classes/competencies:** S2.2 — The FX Market, Participants and Trading Products (the two competency outcomes above). Explicit recall: S2.1 unit interpretation; S2.2 product/counterparty differences.
- **Purpose:** Read executable sides and distinguish displayed movement from a realizable result.
- **Learning objectives / competencies:** Choose correct buy/sell side; separate spread, commission and holding/financing costs. Demonstrate both with fresh material; terminology recognition alone is insufficient.
- **Core concepts:** Two-way quotes; long/short conceptual exchange; entry/exit side; variable spread; commission; rollover concept; indicative versus executable price.
- **Student demonstration:** Choose sides for opening/closing long and short examples and calculate a stated quote difference.
- **Visual / interactive modes:** Two-sided quote board; transaction arrows; cost-reveal comparison.
- **Homework / practical work:** Complete a fictional execution-side ledger with a spread widening and explicit fee assumptions.
- **Targeted remediation:** Using bid to buy; double-counting spread; assuming a displayed last price guarantees a fill. Return to the failed skill and reassess with a changed example, preserving original attempts.
- **Future stage-assessment evidence:** Novel buy/sell quote case with cost reconciliation.
- **Live-teaching purpose:** Have learners act as customer and dealer, then widen quotes and explain the effect. This is a substantive approximately 60-minute facilitated session, with individual follow-up outside it where required.
- **Explicitly deferred / excluded:** Pip-value conversion, order execution depth and risk sizing.

#### S2.4 — Pips, Quote Precision and Price Movement

- **Stage / sequence:** `stage-2`; class 4 of 6; Academy class 12 of 55.
- **Prerequisite classes/competencies:** S2.3 — Bid, Ask, Spread and Trading Costs (the two competency outcomes above). Explicit recall: S2.1 unit rates; S2.3 quote differences.
- **Purpose:** Measure movement using the convention supplied for the instrument.
- **Learning objectives / competencies:** Calculate signed pip movements; distinguish pip, fractional pip and displayed precision. Demonstrate both with fresh material; terminology recognition alone is insufficient.
- **Core concepts:** Typical 0.0001 and JPY 0.01 pip conventions with explicit specification; fractional increments; signed versus absolute movement.
- **Student demonstration:** Calculate ordinary and JPY-style movements and explain a fractional-pip display without applying one decimal rule universally.
- **Visual / interactive modes:** Digit/place-value ruler; movable price markers; worked calculation with retry.
- **Homework / practical work:** Numerical movement worksheet with units, precision and explanation of one correction.
- **Targeted remediation:** Wrong place value; ignoring direction; confusing ticks/fractional pips and pips. Return to the failed skill and reassess with a changed example, preserving original attempts.
- **Future stage-assessment evidence:** Fresh conventional/JPY examples and a supplied nonstandard specification.
- **Live-teaching purpose:** Use keyboard-controlled price movement and compare students’ units before revealing results. This is a substantive approximately 60-minute facilitated session, with individual follow-up outside it where required.
- **Explicitly deferred / excluded:** Account-currency pip value and position sizing until Stage 7.

#### S2.5 — Units, Lots, Leverage and Margin

- **Stage / sequence:** `stage-2`; class 5 of 6; Academy class 13 of 55.
- **Prerequisite classes/competencies:** S2.4 — Pips, Quote Precision and Price Movement (the two competency outcomes above). Explicit recall: S2.2 products; S2.4 movement units.
- **Purpose:** Understand exposure and collateral before any trading exercise.
- **Learning objectives / competencies:** Relate units/lot specifications to exposure; distinguish required margin from maximum possible loss. Demonstrate both with fresh material; terminology recognition alone is insufficient.
- **Core concepts:** Product-specific contract/lot size; notional exposure; leverage ratio; margin as collateral; margin calls/liquidation; loss beyond expected stop under adverse execution.
- **Student demonstration:** Compare two specified exposures at different leverage and explain why lower margin is not lower risk.
- **Visual / interactive modes:** Exposure/collateral comparison scale; adverse-move consequence scenario.
- **Homework / practical work:** Fictional risk-awareness scenarios with given contract sizes and margin assumptions, no personal allocation.
- **Targeted remediation:** Equating margin with risk budget; lot size without specification; viewing high leverage as an advantage. Return to the failed skill and reassess with a changed example, preserving original attempts.
- **Future stage-assessment evidence:** Exposure/margin distinction and safe refusal of an inadequately specified product.
- **Live-teaching purpose:** Hold exposure constant while varying collateral to expose the misconception. This is a substantive approximately 60-minute facilitated session, with individual follow-up outside it where required.
- **Explicitly deferred / excluded:** Risk-budget percentage, pip value, live suitability and portfolio sizing until Stage 7.

#### S2.6 — Sessions, Market Conditions and a Safe Platform Orientation

- **Stage / sequence:** `stage-2`; class 6 of 6; Academy class 14 of 55.
- **Prerequisite classes/competencies:** S2.5 — Units, Lots, Leverage and Margin (the two competency outcomes above). Explicit recall: S2.2 product/feed; S2.3 costs; S2.5 collateral risk.
- **Purpose:** Orient to time, conditions and a demo workspace without promoting a preferred trading hour.
- **Learning objectives / competencies:** Convert session windows with timezone/DST context; verify instrument/account/environment and distinguish observation from order submission. Demonstrate both with fresh material; terminology recognition alone is insufficient.

- **Student demonstration:** Identify an overlap for a supplied date, verify feed/product and choose to observe rather than submit an inadequately understood ticket.
- **Visual / interactive modes:** Date-aware world/session timeline; annotated fictional platform; verification checklist.
- **Homework / practical work:** Session/calendar worksheet plus read-only demo orientation record; no funded account required.
- **Targeted remediation:** Fixed UTC offsets across DST; overlap guarantees; wrong symbol/account; demo results treated as live fills. Return to the failed skill and reassess with a changed example, preserving original attempts.
- **Future stage-assessment evidence:** Date-stamped timing case and environment-verification task; integrated Stage 2 mechanics case.
- **Live-teaching purpose:** Compare seasonal overlap dates and demonstrate a read-only ticket with real submission disabled. This is a substantive approximately 60-minute facilitated session, with individual follow-up outside it where required.
- **Explicitly deferred / excluded:** Order-type execution drills Stage 8; macro inference Stage 6; full risk clearance Stage 7.

## 7. Teaching design, accessibility and Phase C compatibility

Gold-Standard v1 is experience → visualize → predict/interact → explain → apply → demonstrate → check → reinforce → review/homework, used where meaningful. No fixed section count, card count, A–F homework or mandatory widget sequence. Stage 2 emphasizes unit-labeled quote boards, numerical rulers and timezone timelines. Stage 3 uses chart geometry, same-data views and point selection. Stages 4–6 use replay, maps and hypothesis comparison. Stage 7 uses dimensional worksheets and risk-policy cases. Stage 8 uses workflow/ticket simulation. Stage 9 uses behavioral rehearsal. Stage 10 uses rulebooks/data ledgers. Capstone uses independent production of evidence and defense.

Every future class receives a private substantive live guide with prerequisites, hook, terminology, pacing, demonstrations, misconceptions, questions, a poll, a pop-quiz opportunity, a written prompt, screen-share opportunity, recap and transition. These activities are options, not a requirement to cram every tool into each hour. Typical planning allowance is opening/retrieval 5–10 minutes, visual reasoning/demo 10–15, guided practice 15–20, independent transfer/discussion 10–15, recap and next work 5–10; authors adjust to subject while totaling approximately 60 minutes. Capstone uses briefing → independent evidence → defense/debrief instead of teaching answers during assessment. Written/typed defense and adjusted pace must be equivalent alternatives to timed speech.

Use existing Phase C curriculum hooks for explicit instructor-launch polls, pop quizzes, written responses, optional official YouTube, instructor observations and approved after-class handoff. No new classroom authority, auto-launch or automatic academic credit. Screen-sharing TradingView or another approved tool is optional enrichment; deterministic same-data alternatives must exist. Do not require paid subscriptions, live broker credentials, a funded account, live positions or real-money losses. Group sessions may share discussion but each learner produces individually reviewable work; never expose private classmates’ responses.

Permanent Study Guides are individually authored concise reference aids: terminology, diagrams, worked examples, assumptions, common mistakes and self-checks in fresh words. Calculations retain units/specifications. Chart guides pair geometry with tables, time/feed context and inference limits. Private rubrics, answer keys, competency IDs, version/authoring metadata and other students’ records stay out of student payloads. Normal instructor screens use labels; advanced IDs are available privately where useful.

All visuals need text equivalents, labeled numeric values and non-color signals. Click/tap chart tasks need keyboard point selection and table/coordinate alternatives with equal academic requirements. Sorting cannot require dragging. No page-level horizontal overflow at approximately 393px; dense data may have a clearly labeled local scroller plus summary/table alternative. Respect reduced motion and visible focus. Do not grade motor precision, screen size, speed or ability to purchase software as chart competence.

## 8. Practical evidence and future Chart Lab boundary

This design specifies educational contracts, not a Chart Lab implementation. Approved practical records should describe:

- **Example/context:** stable example reference, original/licensed deterministic OHLC or event data, timezone, instrument, feed/product specification, timeframe, visible range and reveal cutoff. Same-data transformations must be reproducible; no invented impossible OHLC.
- **Expected demonstration:** operational annotation rule and allowed alternatives, valid coordinate/label ranges, known uncertainty, required reasoning and disqualifying misconception. Different defensible maps can pass a stated rubric.
- **Student response:** point/region/label selections, accessible equivalent table/coordinates, calculation working, short explanation and pre/post decision record as appropriate.
- **Review/evidence:** class/competency association, knowledge versus practical scope, criterion decisions, feedback, reviewer and immutable attempt/revision provenance privately. Old evidence is never rewritten to match a new rubric.
- **Correction:** failed criterion → specific prerequisite practice → fresh analogous task → review; retain original and revised evidence. An approved completion must not be silently revoked by optional practice.

Stages 3–6 require saving/reviewing deterministic chart responses for eventual practical certification, but **do not require a full live charting platform**. If the existing runtime cannot persist a visual response, later implementation must propose the minimum additive response/review contract; a text box or screenshot claim is not proof the practical runtime exists. An accessible coordinate/table task can demonstrate the same skill if approved and actually reviewed. Stages 8/Capstone similarly require a controlled simulation/event log; an external demo workspace is optional, not an assumed existing HFT feature. Secure uploads, live feeds, arbitrary drawings, replay engines, order simulators and automatic chart scoring are not authorized by blueprint approval.

Classroom attendance or instructor demonstration does not satisfy an individual practical. Future Chart Lab can consume these contexts/responses/rubrics without becoming a second mastery/progression authority. No practical credit until the actual evidence/review pathway is implemented and tested.

## 9. Approved assessment policy and explicit limits

Approved shared rubric dimensions: accuracy; interpretation/assumptions; application; uncertainty/alternatives; safe operation; evidence integrity. Use explicit criterion decisions (demonstrated / needs revision / not observed), not a new aggregate “mastery score.” Existing Stage 1 scores and rules stay untouched. Before authoring a stage assessment, define unseen task variants and deterministic tolerances privately; do not publish final answer banks in lessons.

Approved critical criteria include wrong instrument/account/side or unit causing unintended exposure, size/aggregate limit violation, disabled safety controls, hidden rule changes, fabricated evidence, and acting when required specifications are unknown. Profit cannot compensate. A safety error stops the relevant simulated action, is documented and receives targeted practice plus fresh reassessment; it is not a punitive permanent failure. Minor presentation mistakes do not become capital-critical errors. Instructor rubric must distinguish an error noticed/corrected before submission from an executed unsafe action.

**Approved minimum Capstone evidence pack:** frozen system dossier; three unseen simulated case sequences (one eligible/setup-management case, one no-trade/limit-filter case, one adverse-fill or interruption case); no forced minimum number of trades; complete records for all eligible opportunities; one separate prospective simulated-session record under frozen rules from Stage 10; one targeted new transfer/retest case in Part 3. Cases can incorporate several market conditions without arbitrary multi-month demands. A predefined scenario pack ensures comparable coverage but does not establish statistical profitability. If a rule set legitimately never triggers, provide an additional rule-compatible unseen execution scenario to assess mechanics, clearly distinguished from evidence of strategy frequency/edge. No incentive to trade solely to satisfy a trade quota.

System evaluation may produce negative or inconclusive results. A learner can demonstrate research/process competence by rejecting or suspending an unsupported system and accurately defending that decision; completing Capstone then indicates process capability **with a do-not-deploy conclusion**, not evidence that the rejected strategy should be traded. Section 12.3 explicitly ratifies this graduation meaning. Real-money financial readiness and commercial brokerage authorization remain separate processes.

No numerical win rate, profit amount, return, funded-account challenge or universal live risk percentage is prescribed. Fictional numeric examples support learning, not individualized financial advice. Future instrument-specific/legal claims require current jurisdiction/product sources, not a universal broker assertion.

### 12.1 Scope, cadence and workload

One class normally represents one approximately **60-minute weekly teaching session**, covering one coherent skill or closely related group. Plan **15–30 minutes** Academy review/practice plus **20–45 minutes** purposeful homework; numerical/chart practicals may require **30–60 minutes**. These are planning ranges, not mandatory time-on-task credit or promises of graduation timing. Stage 10 research and Capstone portfolios accumulate across sessions with explicit workload notice. No guaranteed 55-week graduation; reviews, stage integration, holidays and remediation can extend the calendar.

Progress follows approved evidence. Learners can move slower or faster with instructor-approved prerequisite evidence, without automatically expanding the existing default weekly live-lesson allowance. A booked session can become supervised correction, practice or review when the next class is not academically available. Booking authority and academic progression stay separate; delayed review does not waive a gate or cancel the learning relationship. Attendance, payment for extra sessions and speed are not mastery.

### 12.2 Analytical competence and critical terminology literacy

Require chart/timeframe/swing interpretation, explicit and consistent structural rules, observation-versus-hypothesis distinction, alternative explanations, invalidation, execution/liquidity limitations, risk-aware planning and no-trade judgment. Knowing an acronym is not competence.

S6.3 remains within the 55 classes as **required critical terminology literacy**: evaluate a supplied working definition, identify a qualifying example and counterexample/failure, and explain its limits. Adoption of fair-value-gap, order-block, BOS/CHoCH or another community’s methodology in the learner’s own system is **optional**. Equivalent meaningful descriptions and rule-consistent annotations satisfy the same criteria without acronym memorization. The Capstone must not secretly require optional terminology. No chart label proves hidden orders, institutional intent, guaranteed filling/reversal or a profitable edge.

### 12.3 Capstone, minimum evidence and graduation meaning

**HFT Graduate** means the learner has demonstrated an individually reviewed, reproducible trading process in controlled simulation: interpreting market information, making conditional decisions, protecting capital, executing or abstaining appropriately, preserving records, and correcting errors without instructor trade signals. Graduation is **process-based, not profit-based**. It is not financial certification, investment-adviser qualification, guaranteed profitability, a validated commercial edge, funded-account eligibility or authorization to trade live capital. Financial readiness is separate from educational achievement.

Required demonstrations cover market/product/feed context; chart reading; explicit structure; relevant liquidity/context and inference limits; conditional bias or justified neutrality; setup and **NO-TRADE** recognition; entry reasoning; invalidation; stop/fill reasoning; position and aggregate risk calculations; target/management reasoning; discipline; plan adherence; post-trade reconciliation; and ability to explain uncertainty and decisions. R1–R8 in Section 3 trace these requirements to prerequisite instruction and evidence. A winning simulation can fail process criteria; a compliant loss can demonstrate competence.

| Part | Purpose / evidence / student demonstration | Instructor role | Passing / revision |
|---|---|---|---|
| C.1 Defend the Process | Frozen system dossier, prerequisite evidence, fresh mechanics/risk tasks and reasoned rule/analysis defense | Probe provenance and reasoning without giving answers | Readiness criteria demonstrated before independent C.2 operation; targeted correction and fresh checks for unmet criteria |
| C.2 Operate Under Uncertainty | Unseen cases with predecision analysis, plans, risk checks, simulated order/management records, no-trade decisions and complete journals | Observe without entry coaching; halt unsafe action and document intervention | Required operational criteria demonstrated; unresolved criteria remain pending and profit cannot compensate |
| C.3 Review, Correct and Prove the Process | Reconciled audit, honest error analysis, fresh transfer/correction task and final defense | Assess correction/transfer and record criterion-level approval or remaining work | Accessible after a recorded C.2 attempt and debrief even if revision is needed; access is not approval; all required criteria must eventually be demonstrated |

These are three assessment parts, not a guaranteed three-appointment completion. An unresolved safety failure stops affected execution until targeted practice and supervised fresh reassessment; correction discussion remains accessible.

**Approved minimum evidence:** (1) frozen reproducible system dossier including risk, management, behavioral and contingency rules; (2) Stage 10 complete eligible-case testing ledger with costs, limitations and version history; (3) a separate prospective simulated-session record under frozen rules; (4) three unseen Capstone case sequences covering a rule-compatible setup/management opportunity, a genuine no-trade/limit-filter decision, and an adverse-fill/interruption scenario; (5) fresh transfer/correction task and final defense. No profit requirement or arbitrary trade quota. If cases never trigger the learner’s rules, provide an additional compatible controlled execution case rather than compel an invalid trade.

A learner may demonstrate research/process competence by accurately rejecting an unsupported strategy, but the final record must explicitly say **system not cleared for deployment; continue simulation/research**. That outcome cannot replace demonstration of planning/execution mechanics in an appropriate controlled case. Preserve original plans, failed attempts, interventions and revisions. Target affected criteria for reassessment; repeat integration only when the failure undermines integrated performance.
